Reflection on class topics

31 August 2026

To be honest I rarely ever think about my own social identity beyond very surface level things. When I am a stranger to someone, I imagine they draw an idea of what sort of person I may be by the sex, age, skin color, posture, eye contact, weight, gait, and clothing I present. Every day we walk outside into the public we actually present a lot of information about ourselves. The question then becomes, is that information enough to draw any conclusions? I speculate that if someone were to draw all the conclusions they could about me, by the criteria previously mentioned, that for every correct conclusion, they would make an incorrect conclusion. Which is pretty bad. But also pretty good. I think it is pretty interesting that someone might be able to infer that much about me before I even open my mouth. Of course, these are just guesses for myself. For me, maybe some of these qualities work in my favor depending on the person making them. Others, however, may not be so lucky.

One of the most painfully obvious examples is the comments and treatment women routinely get in society. I find it quite disturbing how many women have told me they routinely get provocative comments made at them, while they are doing something as mundane as walking to class, without any sort of effort to draw unwanted attention to themselves. For whatever reason, this is not as big of a topic as it should be. For something that is affecting ~50% of the population, we seem rather unconcerned. Regardless, this is an example of a very common physical trait (appearing female), which can actually work against someone. I offer no solution to the problem or speculation on why it is the way it is, but through this course I have become more aware of this problem. Which I do believe is worth something.

I hope for a future where my daughters (if I am to be blessed with any) may walk the street, and carry out their mundane tasks (or any task for that matter) without feeling threatened by the rude comments of some strange man.
